# Portfolio content references

Research date: 2026-08-19

## Purpose

Review established developer portfolios for content patterns that help employers understand role, work, experience, and technical judgment. The reference set is used for writing principles, not for copying language or structure.

## Reference set

### Bryl Lim

Bryl opens with a direct role statement, names the products he builds, and gives each featured application a one-sentence description of what it does. Work history and stack follow as factual lists rather than promotional claims.

Source: [bryllim.com](https://bryllim.com/)

### Brittany Chiang

Brittany states her specialty, explains the kind of interface work she does, names her current role, and supports the positioning with detailed experience entries. Project descriptions explain the product function before listing technology.

Source: [brittanychiang.com](https://brittanychiang.com/)

### Lee Robinson

Lee uses a plain introduction: role, current work, previous work, years of experience, and the subject he writes about. The language is personal and specific without adding a marketing headline to every section.

Source: [leerob.io](https://leerob.io/)

### Paco Coursey

Paco uses short factual descriptions for current work and projects, followed by a longer personal note that explains what he values in interface work. The technical identity comes from the work and writing rather than a list of adjectives.

Source: [paco.me](https://paco.me/)

### Josh W. Comeau

Josh's article summaries state the exact problem, explain why it matters, and tell the reader what the article covers. The voice is personal, but technical subjects remain concrete.

Source: [joshwcomeau.com](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/)

## Content principles applied

1. State the role and primary technologies in plain language.
2. Explain what each product does before discussing architecture or stack.
3. Use first-person language for personal work and direct verbs for experience.
4. Keep detail when it provides evidence: requirements, system boundaries, tests, deployment, and user flows.
5. Remove unsupported adjectives, contrast slogans, and repeated claims about judgment, quality, or product thinking.
6. Describe AI tools through actual tasks, review steps, and verification practices.
7. Separate measured GitHub data from claims about engineering quality.
8. Use personality in the About section, while keeping hiring information easy to scan.

## Resulting direction

The portfolio should read as a clear account of what Van builds, how the systems work, what he contributed professionally, and how he verifies agent-assisted work. Product thinking should be visible in the project scope and decisions rather than repeated as a label.
